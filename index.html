01 About

Okay, so who is F.Liyanto?

I'm Fatta Putra Liyanto

An Indonesian digital designer born and raised in Surabaya, now creating interactive digital experiences from Melbourne, Australia.

My work sits between UI design, interaction design and front-end development, with a focus on making digital experiences feel clear, responsive and enjoyable to use.

I like work that feels clean without becoming boring, playful without becoming distracting, and functional without losing its personality.

02 What I Do

Where design meets interaction.

I design digital experiences that invite people to interact, explore and enjoy.

Choose one to see how I approach it.

01

UI Design

  • Visual hierarchy
  • Typography
  • Layout systems
  • Responsive UI
  • Accessibility

I shape interfaces around hierarchy, spacing, typography and visual rhythm so information feels easy to understand without making the experience feel generic.

What I notice first

I’m drawn to hierarchy, spacing, typography and the small details that make an interface easier to understand.

How I shape the interface

I start with what the user needs to notice and understand first, then build the interface around that hierarchy.

02

Interaction Design

  • Motion
  • Feedback
  • Micro-interactions
  • Prototyping
  • Interaction states

I think about what happens before, during and after an interaction, using motion and feedback to communicate change while keeping the experience understandable and comfortable to use.

Where movement matters

I like the moments where an interface responds back — transitions, feedback, movement and small interactions that make something feel alive.

How I make it respond

I think about what should happen before, during and after an interaction, then refine timing and movement.

03

Front-End

  • HTML
  • CSS
  • JavaScript
  • Responsive builds
  • Interactive prototypes

I use HTML, CSS and JavaScript to move ideas beyond static screens, testing layouts and interactions directly in the browser.

What I like bringing to life

Front-end lets me take an idea beyond a static design and see how it actually behaves.

How I build it

I usually build progressively: structure first, styling second, then interaction.

04

Web Design

  • Responsive design
  • Information hierarchy
  • Layout adaptation
  • Web interaction
  • Cross-device design

I design web experiences as flexible systems, considering how content should move, resize and simplify across screen sizes.

What I care about across screens

I’m interested in how a website keeps its personality while adapting to different screens.

How I keep it consistent

I design with change in mind from the beginning so the experience remains recognisably the same.

Emotional Reflection Tool 2026

LEGA

  • UI Design
  • Interaction Design
  • Responsive Design
  • Accessibility
  • Front-End

The problem I kept noticing

Emotional reflection can become difficult when the first thing someone is asked to do is name exactly what they feel. LEGA explores whether reflection can begin somewhere simpler, using interaction before asking for precise language.

Reflection without forcing a label

The experience uses sliders, optional notes, emojis, templates and visual timelines to let people describe different qualities of a moment without first choosing a named emotion.

Building a lower-pressure experience

LEGA developed ideas first explored through Pulse Diary into a more practical experience focused on repeated use, accessibility, responsive behaviour and flexible ways to begin a reflection.

Interactive Digital Diary 2026

Pulse Diary

  • UI Design
  • Interaction Design

The thought behind it

Pulse Diary started as an experiment in making digital reflection feel more expressive. I was interested in what could happen when a diary behaved less like a text document and more like an interactive visual experience.

Making reflection feel engaging

I focused on interface behaviour, feedback and visual movement as ways to make reflection feel less passive and more responsive.

Where the idea went next

Pulse Diary became an early conceptual foundation for LEGA, helping shape a more practical reflection experience designed for repeated everyday use.

Nada Nusantara

  • Web Design

Where it began

Nada Nusantara began from my interest in combining Indonesian culture with the kind of digital interaction I enjoy designing.

What I wanted people to experience

The goal was to make discovery feel active, using sound, movement and visual responses to encourage curiosity rather than presenting culture as static information.

Turning sound into interaction

I focused on rhythm, motion and responsive feedback so movement, interaction and audio could work together as one digital experience.
